This reading time calculator estimates how many minutes it takes to read a piece of text based on its word count and a typical reading speed. Paste an article, essay or blog draft and it returns an at-a-glance estimate you can show readers or use to plan content length. It is popular with bloggers, editors, students and speakers who need to gauge how long their writing will take an audience.

How the Reading Time Calculator Works

The calculator counts the words in your text and divides that total by an average silent reading speed — commonly around 200 to 250 words per minute for adults reading general content. The formula is simply word count divided by words per minute, rounded up to the nearest minute so short pieces still show a realistic figure.

Reading speed varies with difficulty, so dense technical material reads slower than a casual blog post. Treat the result as a helpful estimate rather than a stopwatch-exact time, and adjust your expectations for very technical or very light content.

Example: A 1,000-word blog post at 200 words per minute takes 1000 ÷ 200 = 5 minutes to read — the sort of length many sites label "5 min read."
Example: A 3,000-word long-form guide at 250 words per minute works out to 3000 ÷ 250 = 12 minutes, useful for deciding whether to split it into sections.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast does the average person read?

Most adults read silently at roughly 200 to 250 words per minute for everyday material. Speed drops for complex or technical text and rises for easy, familiar content, which is why reading-time estimates are approximate.

How long should a blog post be for SEO?

There is no single magic number, but many well-ranking articles fall in the 1,000 to 2,000-word range because that length lets you cover a topic thoroughly. Focus on fully answering the reader's question rather than hitting an exact word count.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is the word count divided by an assumed reading speed in words per minute, then rounded to the nearest minute. This tool uses a standard adult reading pace so the estimate reflects how long a typical reader would spend.

Why does my reading time seem off?

Estimates assume an average pace, so highly technical writing, poetry or content with lots of numbers may take longer, while skimmable lists read faster. Use the figure as a planning guide and adjust for your specific audience.
